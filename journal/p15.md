# P15: the two sittings where something was actually measured

The lab is [POW-BB mixed-voltage discipline](../chapters/15-pow-bb-mixed-voltage-discipline.md).
It is the only lab in this volume that has been run on hardware, so this is the
only file here describing something that happened on a bench rather than in a
document.

## Friday 2 October 2026: polarity, settled by three controls

Three indicator modules were driven from header pins 11, 13 and 15, BCM GPIO17,
GPIO27 and GPIO22, on a Raspberry Pi 3 Model B Rev 1.2. Grounds went to a
breadboard rail and one jumper from that rail to a Pi ground pin.

What the sitting established, and it is **measured** rather than reasoned:

- **The signal pin is the anode side.** The header pin sources current.
- **High is lit, low is dark**, and a line released to an input is dark and
  reads low.
- **The module needs signal and ground only.** One module had its supply pin
  wired and lit identically with that jumper pulled out.
- Three lit together with no visible dimming.

**Which way round the LED sits is not printed on the board.** That is why the
chapter tells you to settle one module with three controls, high then low then
released, before wiring three. The sitting did exactly that, which is why the
result is a measurement and not an impression.

## The belief this replaced, and what it had already cost

A note had recorded that the module's 2.0 mm housing could not take ordinary
jumpers, and that a dedicated cable or a soldered pigtail was required.

**It was false.** The module carries a 2.54 mm male header beside the housing.
Ordinary jumpers mate with it. A photograph settled it.

By then the belief had already deferred an LED output in one project after three
pins were driven into open air, deferred three status indicators in another, and
caused a third to be designed around bare components that are not in this bin at
all. **Nothing was ever missing.** The cost of an unchecked note is not the note;
it is every decision downstream that quietly routes around an obstacle that is
not there.

## Saturday 3 October 2026: the currents, and a wrong prediction

Each module alone on a source meter, signal to the supply output and ground to
ground, window on the lit stretch:

| | blue | green | yellow | red |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| at 5.000 V | 9.81 mA | 15.98 mA | 13.10 mA | 13.16 mA |
| at 3.300 V | 2.94 mA | 7.39 mA | 6.20 mA | 6.34 mA |
| effective R | 247 ohm | 198 ohm | 246 ohm | 249 ohm |
| derived Vf | 2.57 V | 1.84 V | 1.77 V | 1.72 V |

The fraction kept at 3.3 V is `(3.3 - Vf) / (5 - Vf)` and it matches all four.
Blue keeps 30 per cent, the other three about half. Three modules on the Pi
together draw 16.5 mA at 3.3 V.

**A prediction was made before the measurement and it was wrong.** Green was
expected to sit with blue, on the reasoning that modern green emitters have a
high forward voltage. This green is the older low-forward-voltage type, so it
sits with yellow and red. Its effective resistance is also the odd one out at
198 ohm against about 247 for the other three, most likely a different fitted
resistor.

The chapter records that prediction as wrong rather than presenting the corrected
result as though it had always been expected.

## Why this lab stops at run and not passed

Three of its seven acceptance items are satisfied and written down: the common
ground shown by a module lighting from a host pin, one module's polarity settled
by three controls, and no jumper between the 5 V rail and any header pin at any
point.

Two more are one sitting away, since the semaphore item asks for a program rather
than the manual commands that have stood in for one.

**The rails item needs a voltmeter this bench does not have**, so the chapter
records them as unmeasured with the reason named rather than leaving a blank or,
worse, an estimate.

## What neither sitting captured

Neither recorded the operating system image or the kernel. The board revision is
there, and so is the userspace library version, but not what was running.

That is why the volume now asks for four commands together, and why this lab
cannot reach **passed** on the strength of what it has. "Bookworm" alone does not
tell the next reader what was running.
