# P13: a lab I predicted wrongly, and a clock that is an argument

The lab is [Dual-radio signalling failover](../chapters/13-dual-radio-signalling-failover.md),
written on **Saturday 17 October 2026**, the sixth host side in this volume.

## The prediction, and what the chapter actually said

After P02 and P03 I said in as many words that P13 would be the next lab
worth writing because it drives two modems at once and would be the first
user of the AT engine needing two sessions open together. That was a
prediction made from the lab's title and its place in the table.

It is wrong. P13's host side is not an AT program and opens no session at
all. The primary publisher is `mosquitto_pub` in a shell loop through a
host route, the backup publisher is P02's path with a different payload, and
the thing this lab actually needs written is the **supervisor**: a subscriber
with a clock, two states and one timer. The AT engine is not used here.

Recording that because the shape of the error is familiar. The volume has
already withdrawn three claims that came from a note rather than from the
page it summarised, and this is the same move applied to a chapter: I
reasoned about what a lab needed from its one-line description instead of
reading its steps. The cost was small, ten minutes and a sentence to the
reader, because the reading came before the writing. It would not have been
small the other way round.

## The clock is an argument, not a call

Nothing in the state machine reads a clock. The time arrives as a parameter,
and three things follow that are worth more than the tidiness.

**A replay reaches the same transitions as the run.** The log carries the
times, the replay ticks at one hertz between records exactly as the live
loop does, and the two therefore agree. Without the matching tick they would
differ by up to a second at every fall, and the log would stop being
evidence of what happened.

**The window is testable at its boundary rather than near it.** The suite
asserts that at forty five seconds exactly the primary is late and at forty
five point zero zero one it is missing. No test with a real clock in it can
say that.

**A ten minute run is tested in microseconds**, so the suite runs five of
them and nobody is tempted to skip it. The arithmetic of the outage scene is
checked exactly: thirty four primary records with two minutes of them
missing, six backup records that never stopped, one fall and two rises,
the first rise being the start of the run.

## The refusal that matters is of a healthy run

`verify` reads a log and judges it against the chapter's acceptance test. The
case worth building it for is not a broken run but a perfect one.

A log of a bench where everything worked all afternoon demonstrates nothing
about what happens when an antenna comes off, and it is exactly the log
somebody will produce when they mean to do the antenna test and forget. So
the gate refuses it, in those words: **the state never fell, so the antenna
test is not in this log.**

Four of the five scenes are refused, each for its own reason, and the
workflow checks all four. The one worth keeping is `short-gap`: the primary
misses a single sample, which is inside the window, and nothing falls. A
window of one sample would flip on it. A reader who shortens the window to
make the lab feel more responsive finds that out here rather than in the
field, which is the whole argument for a window of three missed samples
rather than one.

There is a fifth refusal that is not a scene. A log records a run at one
window, and judging it against another makes the fall look late or early, so
the suite judges a forty five second run against a ten second window and
requires the refusal.

## What stays a claim, as of Saturday 17 October 2026

**None of this has been compiled**, and `STATUS.md` leaves P13 at **written**
for that reason.

Two claims will outlive the first green run. Whether forty five seconds is
the right window is a question for a working day of observation, which is
what the chapter asks for and what no amount of generated scenes can answer.
And the acceptance item about routes, that the Pi 4 has one default route
through its modem and the Pi 3 has none through its own, is checked with
`ip route` on two hosts and is not something a supervisor can see. That is
the point of the design: this program sees one stream of tagged records and
nothing else, which is also why it cannot check that item for you.
