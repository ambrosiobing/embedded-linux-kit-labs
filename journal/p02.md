# P02: a power key put on the wrong pin by a note nobody checked

The lab is [NB-IoT PSM/eDRX CoAP field node](../chapters/02-nb-iot-psm-edrx-coap-field-node.md).
Its record is short and it is the one that set the pattern the other three
withdrawals followed.

## The claim, and where it came from

A note recorded that the SIM7020E uses BCM GPIO27 as the module's power key, and
reasoned from there that an indicator on that pin would pulse the modem's power
with every change of state. That is a good reason to move an indicator, and it
reached four files: this chapter, P01's, P03's and the appendix.

**It came from a secondhand summary rather than from the vendor.** Nobody had
opened the page it claimed to summarise.

## The withdrawal, Sunday 4 October 2026

The vendor's own wiki puts PWRKEY on header pin 7, BCM GPIO4, selected by the PWR
jumper and on by default. That is the same pin the SIM7070G uses. **GPIO27 is
free on this board.**

Withdrawn by name in all four files on the same day. Not silently corrected: the
chapters say what they used to say and why it was wrong, because a reader who
remembers the old sentence needs to know which version is current.

## Why this one stings more than the others

It was made **while correcting something else**. The indicator pins were being
moved for a good reason and a second, unchecked reason was added to the pile in
passing. That is the clearest possible demonstration that the habit to fix is
not "be more careful" but "open the page", because care was not the thing in
short supply.

The indicator stayed on BCM16 anyway, and the chapter now gives the only reason
left standing: one assignment safe across all four cellular labs is easier to
hold than four. A second withdrawal was later added to the same paragraph, when
the SIM7600E-H claim fell too, because that paragraph had ended by naming
GPIO27 as a pin that board does claim. See [p01](p01.md).

**That second one was found by searching rather than remembering.** Three places
had been corrected from a list; grepping for the claim found a fourth, inside the
chapter that had already withdrawn its own version of the same error.

## What the same page gave that was worth having

The HAT's NET LED is this lab's second instrument, and the wiki documents four
states:

| Pattern | Means |
|---|---|
| 64 ms on, 800 ms off | no network registered |
| 64 ms on, 3000 ms off | registered |
| 64 ms on, 300 ms off | data moving |
| **off** | **power down, or power-saving sleep** |

For a lab whose entire subject is the power-saving duty cycle, that last state is
the one that matters. **A dark NET LED between wakes is the module doing exactly
what it was asked.** A NET LED that keeps blinking is the sign that power saving
was requested and not granted, which is a far more useful failure signal than
anything the host can infer on its own.

None of that was in the volume before someone read the page.
