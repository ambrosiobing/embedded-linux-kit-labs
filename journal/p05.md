# P05: an acceptance band that turned out to be fair by luck

The lab is [MCC 118 100 kS/s voltage recorder](../chapters/05-mcc-118-100-ks-s-voltage-recorder.md).
Nothing about it was wrong. Its record is here because of what reading the
datasheet properly revealed about a number that had already been written down.

## The reading, Wednesday 7 October 2026

The board's electrical specification, revision 1.1, was read end to end rather
than cited. It confirmed the claim this volume most depends on, that the board
takes GPIO12, GPIO13 and GPIO26 as board-address pins, which is the collision
that sent the cellular labs' indicators to BCM16, 20 and 21.

It also filled in detail the chapters had never carried: 1 Mohm input impedance,
150 kHz small-signal bandwidth, 8 us per channel conversion, a 7168-sample
first-in first-out buffer, SPI mode 1 at up to 10 MHz, 35 mA typical from the
3.3 V supply, and a one-minute warm-up that the chapter had already been
instructing for other reasons.

## One rule sharpened rather than changed

The volume says never exceed plus or minus 10.1 V, which is correct. The
datasheet makes clear what kind of limit that is: **10.1 V is the maximum working
voltage, and plus or minus 25 V is the absolute maximum, power on or power off.**

Keep obeying 10.1. Now also know that a slip to 12 V costs a reading you cannot
trust rather than a part you have lost. Those are different kinds of bad, and a
bench calculation that confuses them makes the wrong call under pressure.

## The part worth recording as luck

P05's acceptance test asks that CH0 read 3.30 V plus or minus 0.05 V. That band
was written **before anyone had read the accuracy table.**

The table gives, for the plus or minus 10 V range, gain error 0.098 per cent of
reading and offset error 11 mV. At a 3.3 V reading the converter's own worst-case
error works out near 14 mV, comfortably inside a 50 mV band.

So the test is fair rather than generous or impossible. **It is also luck.** A
band chosen without the table could as easily have been 10 mV, which the
instrument could not have met, and the lab would have failed for a reason that
had nothing to do with the bench. Recorded as luck rather than quietly presented
as judgement, because the next acceptance band written that way might not land.

## The gap this lab cannot close on its own

P05 can reach **run**. It cannot reach **passed**, and no amount of bench time
changes that.

Its acceptance test asks that a multimeter reading of the 3.3 V rail and the
board's own CH0 column agree, and that both are written down. There is no
multimeter here. The board cannot witness itself: the entire point of that item
is a second, independent instrument disagreeing with the one under test.

That makes a cheap multimeter **the single purchase that unblocks the most
acceptance tests in this volume**, which is a more useful thing to know than any
of the specifications above.

## One footnote, left open rather than resolved

The throughput table notes that the highest throughput "may be achieved by using
a Raspberry Pi 3 B+". This volume puts the board on a Pi 4. Nothing says the Pi 4
is worse and the note dates from 2019, so it is recorded as **open** rather than
as a correction. If P05 ever misses its sample count, the host is a thing to
vary before the code is suspected.
